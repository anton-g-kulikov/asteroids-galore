Music and sound

The game's music and every sound effect on one page. The music is one synthwave loop written with Tone.js: its tempo follows the ship's speed, and four more layers come in over the pad as a mission speeds up, a slow lead melody last. Press Play and mission 1 flies by itself at the game's real speed; play any sound effect on its own or over the music. The sounds proposed for story mode's screens have a panel of their own.

Holding to choosepad + arpeggio

Hold a side for 1.2 seconds. While the ring fills, the pad swells on the root and fifth and the arpeggio climbs four steps; letting go early stops it at once, with no other sound. Both sides sound the same, and the landing has no third in it, so it is neither major nor minor.

The manifestbell, piano, pad

Each count that is not zero sounds once as it appears, 0.9 s apart: ON EARTH is the bell chord of a crew member saved, LEFT BEHIND the two piano notes of one left behind, DEAD the low chord of a hit. Then one bell note for the ending's lamp. So a full ship is the bell alone. A fourth count takes its turn after ON EARTH: BY ANOTHER SHIP is the bell chord an octave lower, and MISSING passes in silence, since nobody knows.

Under it, the end screen's slow bass, as after a run in arcade.

Lost at the Heartsilence

The boat's destruction is the last sound. The manifest comes up with no music and no sound for its counts or its lamp: the one silent screen in the game. The other version to hear: after the silence a channel opens, nobody speaks, and it closes.

Layers

Play starts a layer now, whatever the speed, and works with nothing else playing: the tempo then follows the speed slider. Pause silences it. Press the lit button again to hand the layer back to the run, where it comes in or leaves only at a chord change, every two bars.

Lead melody

Press on an empty square to place a note and drag right to hold it longer; press a note to remove it. One square is a beat. The lead plays one note at a time, so a new note cuts the one it overlaps. Tinted squares are notes of the chord underneath; every row is in A minor. The octave buttons move the whole melody an octave down or up; the text below always shows the notes as they sound. On a phone, swipe the chord row to scroll.

Play melody plays the lead alone, all sixteen bars in a loop from the eight bars shown, at 113 BPM, the tempo where the lead comes in during a run. Changes are heard on the next pass.

Piano:

The lead is a piano, synthesized like every sound on this page. Long reverb: a 7 s reverb with an echo a dotted eighth note long. Longer echo: echoes a dotted quarter note apart that die away slowly, in a smaller room. More reverb: a 12 s reverb, more of it than of the note, and no echo. The echoes keep time with the music at any tempo. The game still plays the synth lead.

The text is what the game's music code reads: [start, note, length] in sixteenth notes.

What happens in a run

When the layers come in

Time from the start of each mission at the game's speed (start speed plus 1.3× over the target, src/game/tuning.ts), without slow motion. The lead then waits for the arpeggio and for the start of an eight-bar half, so it comes in up to about 20 seconds after its time here.

What to listen for

  • Is code-made music good enough to keep, or is a recorded track the better route?
  • Does the tempo rise feel right? It runs from 96 BPM at 1× to 140 BPM at about 4.1×.
  • Do the layers come in at the right moments in the missions you play most?
  • With the music on, is a hit and a crew member left behind still clear?
  • Does the loop wear thin over a long mission 7?

Numbers to tune

  • Key: A minor, Am–F–C–G, two bars each. The game's save chord is C major, so it sits inside the same notes.
  • Tempo: 96 BPM at 1×, plus 14 BPM for each 1× of speed, at most 140. 90 BPM between missions.
  • Lead instrument: a piano, on trial here in three sets of effects; the synth lead is what the game plays today.
  • Lead: a sixteen-bar melody (two eight-bar halves) on top of everything, and the last layer in: from 2.2×, only once the arpeggio has played 4 bars, and always from the start of a half, so the melody is never joined in the middle. It plays at most 32 bars at a stretch (the melody twice), fading out over bars 31 and 32, then pauses for 16 bars before starting again from the top. When it comes in it starts the melody from bar 1, wherever the loop is, so its 32 bars are the melody played through twice and the fade falls on the melody's bars 15–16. Each time through takes the next turn, on the owner's word: the first in octaves 2–3 on the piano with the long reverb, the second and third in octaves 3–4 with the longer echo, the fourth as the first again. So a stretch is 2–3 then 3–4; after the pause, 3–4 then 2–3; after the next, 3–4 twice. Played alone or switched on by hand, it uses the octave and piano shown in the editor. (The game plays the synth lead, alternating 3–4 and 4–5 by stretch, today.)
  • Layer order: each layer waits for the one under it. The arpeggio comes in only after the drums have played 4 bars, and the lead only after the arpeggio has played 4 bars. At full speed from a standstill: drums at bar 1, arpeggio at bar 5, lead at bar 9.
  • Layers: bass from 1.3× in mission 1 and from the first bar in missions 2–7, drums from 1.7×, arpeggio from 2.0×; sixteenth-note hats from 2.8×. The pad's filter opens with speed.
  • A rest: at most once every sixteen bars, in the last of them, one of the bass, drums or arpeggio drops out for one bar and comes back on the downbeat. Only a layer that has played at least sixteen bars since it came in can rest, and never the same one twice running.
  • Start screen: silent until the note is tapped, then the whole soundtrack at 96 BPM, the tempo of 1× (it was 110). The slider under the player tries other tempos. Every layer plays there whatever its speed threshold, each waiting its turn: pad, bass and drums from bar 1, the arpeggio at bar 5, the lead at bar 9. The pad stays as bright as at 2×, and the fast hi-hats of 2.8× stay out. A run starts from it without a break: the layers leave at their chord changes as the speed drops to the run's, and every mission opens with a 1.2 s fly-in.
  • End screen after the ship is destroyed: the music fades over 1.5 s, then the bass alone plays one long note every 6 seconds (A, F, C, G in turn) at 60 BPM, the first after 2 seconds, until the next run.
  • Story mode screens (proposed, not built): a call for each of five voices as its name appears, the lines starting 0.3 s later at 60 characters a second over an open channel; a 1.2 s hold with the pad swelling and four arpeggio steps; a landing without a third; the manifest's counts 0.9 s apart.
  • Slow motion: tempo down to 60%, new notes up to five semitones lower, everything muffled, back in 1.4 s.
  • Music level: −12 dB, with a limiter at −1 dB. The sounds play at the game's own level.
