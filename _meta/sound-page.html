Music and sound

The game's music and every sound effect on one page. The music is one synthwave loop written with Tone.js: its tempo follows the ship's speed, and four more layers come in over the pad as a mission speeds up, a slow lead melody last. Press Play and mission 1 flies by itself at the game's real speed; play any sound effect on its own or over the music.

Layers

A layer comes in or leaves only where the chord changes, every two bars, so it always lands on the beat.

Lead melody

Press on an empty square to place a note and drag right to hold it longer; press a note to remove it. One square is a beat. The lead plays one note at a time, so a new note cuts the one it overlaps. Tinted squares are notes of the chord underneath; every row is in A minor. The octave buttons move the whole melody an octave down or up; the text below always shows the notes as they sound. On a phone, swipe the chord row to scroll.

Play melody plays the lead alone, all sixteen bars in a loop from the eight bars shown, at 113 BPM, the tempo where the lead comes in during a run. Changes are heard on the next pass.

The text is what the game's music code reads: [start, note, length] in sixteenth notes.

What happens in a run

When the layers come in

Time from the start of each mission at the game's speed (start speed plus 1.3× over the target, src/game/tuning.ts), without slow motion. The lead then waits for the arpeggio and for the start of an eight-bar half, so it comes in up to about 20 seconds after its time here.

What to listen for

  • Is code-made music good enough to keep, or is a recorded track the better route?
  • Does the tempo rise feel right? It runs from 96 BPM at 1× to 140 BPM at about 4.1×.
  • Do the layers come in at the right moments in the missions you play most?
  • With the music on, is a hit and a crew member left behind still clear?
  • Does the loop wear thin over a long mission 7?

Numbers to tune

  • Key: A minor, Am–F–C–G, two bars each. The game's save chord is C major, so it sits inside the same notes.
  • Tempo: 96 BPM at 1×, plus 14 BPM for each 1× of speed, at most 140. 90 BPM between missions.
  • Lead: a sixteen-bar melody (two eight-bar halves) on top of everything, and the last layer in: from 2.2×, only once the arpeggio has been playing, and always from the start of a half, so the melody is never joined in the middle. It plays at most 32 bars at a stretch (the melody twice), fading out over bars 31 and 32, then pauses for 16 bars before starting again from the top. It alternates octaves: 3–4 the first time it comes in, 4–5 after the pause, then 3–4 again, and so on through the run. It stops between missions and never takes the one-bar rest.
  • Layers: bass from 1.3× in mission 1 and from the first bar in missions 2–7, drums from 1.7×, arpeggio from 2.0×; sixteenth-note hats from 2.8×. The pad's filter opens with speed.
  • A rest: at most once every sixteen bars, in the last of them, one of the bass, drums or arpeggio drops out for one bar and comes back on the downbeat. Only a layer that has played at least sixteen bars since it came in can rest, and never the same one twice running.
  • Start screen: silent until the note is tapped, then the drone, which is the pad alone at 90 BPM with its filter closed, as between missions. A run starts from it without a break, and every mission opens with a 1.2 s fly-in.
  • End screen after the ship is destroyed: the music fades over 1.5 s, then the bass alone plays one long note every 6 seconds (A, F, C, G in turn) at 60 BPM, the first after 2 seconds, until the next run.
  • Slow motion: tempo down to 60%, new notes up to five semitones lower, everything muffled, back in 1.4 s.
  • Music level: −12 dB, with a limiter at −1 dB. The sounds play at the game's own level.
